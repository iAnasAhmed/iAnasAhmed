Customer economics

The direction is right. Returning-customer rate rose from 13% in October 2025 to a steady 19–24% through 2026, and your best repeat customers have spent EGP 5,030–5,805 across three to six orders. A customer who comes back three times is worth roughly 3× your CAC ceiling. You have proof the product retains — you just have no mechanism that asks people to come back.

The Delta is under-served

  • Cairo + Giza + 6th of October = EGP 1.84m, 66% of revenue, from ~22% of Egypt's population.
  • Dakahlia is your fourth-biggest region at EGP 206k. That is the Mansoura studio's halo — physical presence converts. It is a template, not an accident.
  • Alexandria (EGP 241k) under-indexes versus its size and income. No local pop-up, no local creator.
  • Upper Egypt is nearly absent — Minya, Sohag, Asyut, Aswan, Qena together are EGP 55k, under 2%. That is 25 million people.
  • Matrouh does EGP 34,005 on 8 orders — an average order of over EGP 4,250. Summer-coast, high-basket buyers. There is a North Coast seasonal play hiding in that row.

Data hygiene is costing you deliveries

Your city field currently contains, as separate values: Cairo, القاهره, القاهرة, New Cairo, New cairo, Egypt, 1, and blank. Twenty orders worth EGP 40,920 have no region at all.

  • Replace the free-text city field with a governorate dropdown plus an area dropdown at checkout. This is the single highest-return change to your checkout — it cuts failed deliveries, which in a COD market is cash.
  • Make phone number required and validated (01[0125]\d{8}). Your customer records show null phones; a courier who cannot call is a courier who returns the parcel.
  • Add a WhatsApp order confirmation before dispatch. Confirming COD orders before they ship is the standard Egyptian RTO fix.

The tailwinds are real

  • Disinflation is a margin gift. Inflation fell from ~38% (Sep 2024) to 13.4% (Feb 2026) with institutions forecasting 8–10% by end-2026 and the central bank targeting 7% ±2.11 Your input costs stabilise before your prices have to come down.
  • The pound stabilising kills the importers' advantage slowly, not quickly. Anyone importing finished prestige makeup still eats 30% duty + 14% VAT + 5% excise on CIF value.8 A locally made prestige-quality product has a structural 40–50% landed-cost edge over Dior at the same shelf.
  • Egypt is the second-largest cosmetics consumer in Africa (81,000 tonnes in 2024, after Nigeria) and one of its leading manufacturers.13 AfCFTA and COMESA give you preferential access to a 1.5-billion-person market from a Cairo warehouse.
  • Regulation is EU-shaped, which helps you. Egypt's cosmetic rules are largely aligned to EU Regulation 1223/2009 and notification runs through the EDA's EgyCosm system.9 Clearing EDA is an export credential, not just a local hurdle.
  • There is no Sephora in Egypt. Global prestige reaches Egyptian consumers mostly through parallel-import e-tailers. The prestige shelf is unclaimed.

The headwinds are also real

  • Purchasing power is thin and unequal. The middle class is defined at EGP 5,000–25,000 of monthly household income, and the top 10% of households hold 28.6% of disposable income.11 Your EGP 1,500 blush is a genuine luxury purchase for most of the country.
  • Volume fell while value rose. Through the 2024 devaluation Egyptian beauty grew in pounds and shrank in units, with mass outperforming premium.12 You are selling into a market that has learned to trade down.
  • COD is the tax you cannot avoid. 40–70% of Egyptian e-commerce transactions are cash-on-delivery depending on category,10 and your own orders run ~85% pending-payment. That is working capital locked in transit and a 25–35% RTO exposure.
  • Local competition is organised. Nefertari has 100+ stores and reported 35% growth in 2024; Beesline holds 8–10% of the organic sub-segment.7 They own the natural/clean position at scale.
  • Dupe culture is the ceiling. Globally, masstige gained five points of share in five years, and the mid-mass dupe segment is a USD 4.6bn business in 2026.16 Egyptian shoppers see the same TikToks.

The landed-cost wall your international competitors hit

Illustrative, on a USD 20 CIF prestige lipstick

A EUR/USD-priced prestige lipstick arriving in Egypt legally is marked up roughly 1.56× before anyone takes a margin: 30% customs duty, 5% excise on cosmetics as a luxury good, then 14% VAT on the duty-inclusive value.8 Add importer and retailer margin and a USD 20 CIF product lands on shelf near USD 55–70. This is your moat. You can spend genuinely prestige money on formula and packaging and still undercut them — but only if you manufacture at or into Egypt rather than importing finished goods yourself.

The strategy, in one page

Everything above narrows to a single choice about what kind of company this is.

The positioning

The pharmacist's colour house. Prestige pigment, clinical formula, made for Egyptian skin.

Nobody else in this market can credibly say all three. Nefertari owns natural. The parallel importers own foreign prestige. The mass brands own cheap. Nobody owns "formulated by a clinical pharmacist, pigmented like a luxury house, and matched to Egyptian undertones." That is a defensible position because it is true, it is hard to copy, and it converts your biography into the product claim.

Entry buys the customer. Core repeats. Signature carries the margin and the brand. Right now Entry is 12% of the last 90 days and has no defined path into Core — that is the missing conveyor.

The wedge

Own the flush, not the lip

Blush and bronzer posted double-digit gains globally in 2026 and lip liner was the single fastest-growing makeup segment.2 Your own data already agrees: a blush you launched eleven months ago is a quarter of the business. Lipstick is where you came from; cheek and lip-line is where the category is going, and it is where you already have proof.

The moat

Shade truth for Egyptian skin

Global prestige shade ranges are built on Western undertone maps. You have a professional makeup studio, a pharmacist's formulation vocabulary and thousands of Egyptian faces of data. Publish an undertone system — name it, teach it, make every product page answer "is this shade mine?" This is the asset a competitor cannot buy.

What to stop doing

  • Stop running two prices for one formula. The 860/980 lipstick split and the 1,125/1,350/1,500 blush split teach customers to wait for the cheap one.
  • Stop discounting the newest products. Sun Kissed at 18.3% and Güzel Travel at 40% off are training your best-margin customers to devalue your launches.
  • Stop launching a hero with nothing beside it. Every launch needs a companion product on the same page, so the second item is one tap away instead of a second visit.
  • Stop treating international traffic as noise. 4,100 Gulf and diaspora sessions with no shipping option is a closed door with people knocking on it.
  • Stop letting the trough happen. March–June has no plan. Build the always-on offer before you build the next launch.

The Travel Duo problem

Your EGP 400 Travel Duo took 423 orders over the year — more order-lines than any other product — and it is your cheapest thing. Run it through the model at an AOV of 480 (400 + shipping) and it is close to, or below, break-even once cash-on-delivery failure and acquisition cost are included.

That does not mean kill it. It means stop treating it as a product and start treating it as a paid acquisition channel with a job: every Travel Duo box must contain a shade-matched offer that moves that buyer to a EGP 980 or EGP 1,500 purchase within 30 days. If it does that even 20% of the time, it is the best-value customer acquisition you have. If it does not, it is a loss leader with no leader.

The COD tax, quantified

Set the COD share slider to 85% (your reality) and then to 55%, and watch the bottom line. Every point you shift from cash to prepaid removes a return-to-origin risk of 25–35%10 and frees working capital that is currently sitting in couriers' vans.

  • Pay-now discount: 5% off for card or wallet payment costs less than one failed COD parcel.
  • Paymob is ~2.75% + EGP 3; Fawry runs 1.5–2.5%.21 Both are cheaper than a 22% RTO rate.
  • WhatsApp confirmation before dispatch is the single most effective RTO reducer in this market and costs nothing but a person's time.
  • Free shipping above EGP 1,500 pushes AOV toward the Signature band and pays for itself in mix.
