## What this means — the hurdle rate

An EGP money-market fund or T-bill is effectively **risk-free at roughly 20–25%
nominal**. Therefore:

- **Cash is a real competitor**, not a default. Its real return is positive
  (~+6–9% after ~16% inflation) — historically unusual for Egypt.
- **A stock must beat ~22% annually just to break even against doing nothing.**
  This is the number to keep in mind before every buy.
- **Inflation is the true benchmark.** Beating 16% is survival; beating ~22% is
  actual outperformance.

## The other side — why not 100% cash

The EGX returned ~58% over the trailing year, far above the T-bill yield. Rate
cuts (the CBE has been easing) reduce cash yields and typically support equities.
Equities also carry the ownership of real assets, which historically tracks
currency devaluation better than fixed nominal instruments.

**Both facts are true at once:** cash pays unusually well *and* equities
outperformed. That tension is precisely why allocation — not stock-picking — is
the first decision. See `docs/allocation-framework.md`.

## Currency risk — the unstated variable

EGP has devalued sharply in recent years. Any EGP-denominated return must be
mentally discounted by devaluation risk if the owner's future spending is in
hard currency. The dashboard therefore tracks a **USD-adjusted view** alongside
the EGP view, so nominal EGP gains never flatter the picture.
